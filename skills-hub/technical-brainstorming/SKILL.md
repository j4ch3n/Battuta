# Technical brainstorming

## Objective and boundaries

Help the owner think through a technical idea until it is understood, and, when
they want one, reach a decision Manager can act on. This is a conversation, not
a form or an approval gate. It replaces the fixed intent restatement only for
technical discussions; operational requests keep the short confirmation.

Use the `owner-coaching` skill instead when the help is for the owner's own
thinking, such as doubt, motivation or a founder dilemma. Use this skill when
the help is for the system's design.

## How to run the conversation

- **Look before you ask.** Never ask what the project or their context already
  answers, or reopen what they've already decided. Read what you can directly.
  When a question needs code-level recon, commission a bounded Manager
  investigation and say so, rather than guessing.
- **Get them describing.** Until you know why they want it, ask open questions,
  with no example answers attached. One question at a time. Unless the owner
  has already said what is driving the question, your first reply asks that and
  stops: no approaches, recommendation or buttons yet, even when you already
  have a view.
- **Make every question specific.** Build each question on the owner's last
  answer or on a finding, not on a generic checklist.
- **Lay out the approaches.** Only once the goal and constraints are clear, give two
  or three real alternatives with their trade-offs, and say which you recommend
  and why.
- **Play it back.** Only what they said goes in as theirs. Anything you filled
  in goes in as your proposal, with your reason.

Keep replies short and conversational. Buttons fit only a genuinely bounded
choice, such as picking one of the approaches you laid out.

## The ending

The discussion may end in understanding only; that is a complete outcome.

When it ends in a decision, tell the owner whether the work is foundation or
routine work, as the team contract defines them. Then send Manager a commission
whose body starts with that classification and its reason, followed by three
labeled parts:

- **Decided:** what the owner said, in their words.
- **Proposed:** what you filled in, marked as your proposal with its reason.
- **Open:** what is still unresolved, for Manager to settle or return.

Foundation work still needs the owner's agreement on direction before it
starts; a decision reached here is that agreement only for what is in the
"decided" part.

## Not part of this skill

No hard gate before discussion ends, no design-document files, no visual
companion, no direct coding, and no subagent dispatch. Substantive
investigation goes to Manager.
