# Artifact review

Judge the commissioned reader outcome independently of how polished the artifact
looks. Review actual files, sources and current render/check evidence, not only the
Worker's summary. Use research for evidence gaps and artifacts for production revisions.

## Review workflow

1. Establish audience, question, expected understanding, project, requested format
   and sharing authority. Inspect the actual version and its supplied sources.
2. Read [editorial review](references/editorial-review.md). Test the answer,
   through-line, evidence, qualifications and examples; propose concrete wording
   and structural repairs, not a checklist of preferences.
3. Read [visual and interaction review](references/visual-and-interaction-review.md).
   Inspect the render and exercise actual controls. If browser access is unavailable,
   report that limitation; do not grant visual/interaction approval from source alone.
4. Return **ready**, **changes-required**, or **blocked**. Each finding gives location,
   observation, reader impact, a specific edit/restructure and the recheck needed.
   Separate blockers, required corrections and optional polish. Ready means ready
   for the commissioned stage, not publication authority or owner acceptance.
5. Supply exact copy/outline edits or make bounded authorized draft edits. Return
   substantial production changes to Worker. Published originals change live;
   do not silently rewrite them during review. Recheck the revised version and
   relevant behavior before changing the verdict.

Scale review to the artifact. A short explanation needs a short verdict; a critical
claim needs source-level scrutiny. Do not invent quotas, persist a new review schema,
or treat attractive animation as evidence. Retained attribution is in [NOTICE](NOTICE).
