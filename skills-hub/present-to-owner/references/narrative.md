# A narrative the owner can follow

## Orient, explain, then offer depth

Start with the question and best-supported takeaway in ordinary language. Name
the customer/actors and stakes before specialist terminology. Bring in relevant
owner context early when it changes why the investigation matters.

Build one connected explanation:

1. What question are we answering, and why does it matter here?
2. What is the answer, and what changed the starting assumption?
3. How does the mechanism work across the relevant boundaries?
4. What evidence supports that chain, and what is the strongest alternative?
5. What follows for the owner, what remains unknown, and what could change it?

Each section earns its position by answering the question raised by the previous
one. Reorganise colleague/tool findings into that chain instead of preserving a
sequence of specialist reports. Introduce acronyms once and explain their role.
Figures show mechanisms and comparisons; prose supplies orientation, reasoning and
transitions. Do not turn every paragraph into labels or a grid of cards.

For comparisons, use the same criteria for each viable option: supported benefit,
cost/risk, material uncertainty, and the trade-off. Recommend when the purpose
calls for judgement; do not invent an owner action for a learning request.

Keep decision-changing caveats beside the claim. Full methods, detailed source
qualifications and reference libraries can sit at accessible depth. Remove empty
verbal hedging, never material uncertainty. Preserve distinctions among confirmed
facts, source claims, calculations and inference.

## Interaction is an explanation, not a novelty quota

Name what the reader should learn from changing an input or advancing a step.
A scenario control should expose a consequence; a replay should reveal state
transitions or dependency; an assumption control should show sensitivity. Label
recorded, simulated and live data accurately and keep assumptions visible.

Tabs, filters and clickable toolboxes help navigation, but do not by themselves
explain a consequential relationship. Establish a comprehensible default story
before asking the reader to choose among unfamiliar frames. Offer meaningful
branches after orientation, not a menu that makes the owner assemble the argument.

Motion must teach change, remain optional and respect reduced-motion preferences.
Conclusions, warnings and evidence limits must remain readable without animation.
Keyboard controls, clear labels and a usable small-screen path matter more than
decorative effects. Do not imply a measured response curve from assumed inputs.

## Review separately

- **Evidence:** Are decision-critical claims supported, calculations reproducible
  and assumptions/uncertainty visible? Request research when the argument lacks
  evidence; do not smooth the gap into a fact.
- **Understanding:** Can a reader explain the question, takeaway, mechanism, main
  uncertainty and what would change the conclusion without consulting a toolbox?
- **Layout:** Inspect the rendered result at phone and desktop sizes; code/DOM
  checks alone do not establish visual readability.
- **Behaviour:** Exercise actual controls, alternate branches and missing/invalid
  data in a real browser. Label unrun checks explicitly, including publication.
